# Debt, Obligations & Legacy Liabilities

**Purpose:** Resolve every financial claim the old federal state leaves behind — debt, pensions, entitlements, contracts — without creating a successor government that can be held liable for them.

**Core principle:** _The Confederation inherits no debt and assumes no obligation. Claims are settled once, at transition, by explicit rule. No bailouts, no inflation, no perpetual liability._

---

**No successor can carry this.** The settlement must be decisive.

## 2. Guiding Rules (The Five Settlement Principles)

1. **No repudiation of private holders without compensation.** Marketable Treasuries are paid at face value in coin or asset transfer. The Confederation does not default on private creditors.
2. **No assumption of intragovernmental IOUs.** Trust fund "assets" are accounting entries, not claims on future taxpayers. They are extinguished, and beneficiaries are transitioned to county mutuals.
3. **No unfunded pension transferred.** Accrued benefits are honored via funded trusts; future accrual stops at transition.
4. **No contingent guarantee survives.** Deposit insurance, mortgage guarantees, and pension guarantees are wound down or privatized.
5. **No perpetual liability.** Every settlement is one-time, time-bounded, and funded from asset sales + seigniorage + a temporary transition levy.

## 3. Public Debt Held by the Public (~$27T)

### 3.1 Settlement Mechanism

| Step               | Action                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Freeze**      | On Decree Day, all Treasury issuance stops. Existing securities remain valid claims.                                                                                                   |
| **2. Verify**      | Holders register claims with the Transition Trust (digital + paper).                                                                                                                   |
| **3. Pay**         | Claims paid at face value + accrued interest, in one of: (a) coin, (b) federal asset transfer (land, buildings, spectrum), (c) Transition Trust notes (5-year, backed by asset sales). |
| **4. No rollover** | No new federal debt issued. Maturity ladder honored from Trust assets.                                                                                                                 |

### 3.2 Funding the Payoff

| Source                                    | Est. Value    | Timing      | Notes                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Quick Wins (Months 3–12)**              | **$1.0–1.5T** | Months 3–12 | SPR crude ($50–60B), helium ($1–2B), non-operational surplus buildings ($500B), Treasury gold revaluation ($500B), seized assets ($10–20B) |
| **Federal land sales (surplus BLM/USFS)** | ~$2–5T        | Years 1–5   | Non-park, non-wilderness parcels                                                                                                           |
| **Federal buildings (surplus)**           | ~$500B        | Years 1–3   | DC, regional offices                                                                                                                       |
| **Spectrum auctions**                     | ~$500B        | Year 0–1    | FCC-held licenses                                                                                                                          |
| **Strategic reserves (remaining)**        | ~$50B         | Year 0–1    | Minerals, other reserves                                                                                                                   |
| **Gold revaluation (additional)**         | ~$200B        | Year 0–1    | Treasury gold at market (beyond Quick Wins)                                                                                                |
| **Seigniorage (coin)**                    | ~$50B/yr      | Years 0–5   | Mint profit during transition                                                                                                              |
| **Transition levy**                       | ~$500B/yr × 5 | Years 0–5   | Temporary, on high-income/high-wealth (see §3.3)                                                                                           |
| **Asset sales total**                     | **~$5–8T**    |             | Covers ~3–6% of explicit debt + accrued liabilities                                                                                        |

### 3.3 The Gap (Honest Accounting)

Asset sales + levy cover ~$5–8T of the **$27T public debt held by the public**. The remainder of the public debt, plus transition cohort benefits (5 yr), contractor claims, and contingent wind-down, must be funded by the Transition Trust.

**The $75–80T unfunded entitlement PV and ~$4–5T state/local pension PV are NOT funded by the Trust.** Only the transition cohort (current retirees + near-retirees 55+) receives Trust-funded benefits for 5 years. Workers <55 transition to county DC plans/health mutuals with no federal backstop.

**Recommended: Tiered Haircut by Holder Class** (closes the coupon gap; makes the geopolitical bet explicit)

| Holder Class                                                         | Est. Holdings | Treatment                                                                                                                                         | Haircut Savings  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Federal Reserve (SOMA)**                                           | ~$5T          | **Full extinguishment on Decree Day** — intragovernmental accounting entry; no external creditor                                                  | **$5T**          |
| **Foreign official (central banks, sovereign wealth)**               | ~$7–8T        | **60–75% haircut**; remainder → **20-yr Trust notes (1%)** or coin at face (holder choice). No legal forum; dollar reserve status ends by design. | **$4.2–6T**      |
| **Domestic institutional** (mutual funds, pensions, banks, insurers) | ~$11–13T      | **40–50% haircut**; remainder → **20-yr Trust notes (1%)**. Principle 1: partial payment + notes = compensation.                                  | **$4.4–6.5T**    |
| **Domestic retail** (≤$500k/household)                               | ~$1–2T        | **Opt-in swap at par** (D-10 program): LVT credits, TANs, or Trust notes. No haircut.                                                             | **$0**           |
| **Total haircut savings**                                            | —             | —                                                                                                                                                 | **~$13.6–17.5T** |

**Resulting Trust note principal**: ~$27T – $13.6–17.5T haircut = **~$9.5–13.4T** (public debt remainder) + transition cohort benefits (~$3.3–6.3T net of vet savings) + contractor claims (~$0.5T) + contingent wind-down (~$2–4T) = **~$15.3–24.3T total Trust note principal**.

**Coupon arithmetic (20-year amortization)**: $15.3–24.3T × 1% / 20 = **$76–121B/yr**.

- Reading A (0.5% of LVT revenue): $2.5–9B/yr → **17–97× shortfall**
- Reading B (0.5% of land value): $125–175B/yr → **1.0–2.3× coverage** (Reading B covers fully at both ends)

**Design note**: The coupon gap is **closed** via 20-year amortization + Reading B assessment. The 20-year Trust notes reduce annual coupon to $76–121B/yr, fully covered by the $125–175B/yr assessment with margin.

**Principle 1 tension — and the geopolitical bet**: Foreign official holders are market "private holders." A 60–75% haircut is repudiation. The design accepts this **as an intentional feature**, not a bug:

- **No successor state = no forum to sue.** The Confederation has no courts foreign creditors can access.
- **Dollar reserve status ends by design.** Foreign official holdings are the mechanism of dollar hegemony; repudiating them dissolves that architecture permanently.
- **No central bank, no standing army, no borrowing capacity.** The Confederation cannot project force or print money to service external debt. Repudiation is the only coherent option given the institutional reality.
- **Allies (Japan, UK, EU) take the same hit as adversaries.** No exceptions — the haircut is universal for foreign official holders.
- **The Confederation cannot borrow internationally for a generation.** This is accepted: international trade will be conducted county-to-county or compact-to-compact, settled in specie or barter, not sovereign debt instruments (see §11 and vestigial-functions.md §1).

This is not an unintended consequence. It is the design's explicit rejection of imperial monetary architecture.

**Alternative (cleaner but harder)**: Full extinguishment of Fed + foreign official (~$12–13T); domestic institutional 50% haircut; retail protected. Trust note principal ~$10–15T. Coupon $100–150B/yr. Reading B covers cleanly. Diplomatic rupture total.

### 3.4 Voluntary Conversion Premium (Equity-Driven Transition Levy)

Instead of a purely punitive wealth levy, high-income/high-wealth payers may **opt in** to a **Voluntary Conversion Premium**:

- **Mechanism**: Payer directs their transition levy payment (or a portion thereof) into newly chartered **county infrastructure mutuals** — grid, fiber, rail, ports, water systems — receiving **proportional equity stakes** in those mutuals.
- **Valuation**: Equity allocated at mutual charter valuation (independent appraisal). Levy payment = equity purchase at par.
- **Rights**: Equity carries standard mutual member rights (governance, dividends, capital appreciation). No special privileges.
- **Liquidity**: Equity tradable on county mutual secondary markets after **Month 12** (accelerated from Year 3 to provide immediate, liquid, income-generating domestic safe haven against capital flight during Years 0–2).
- **Voluntary**: Payer chooses standard levy (pure tax) or Conversion Premium (equity). No coercion.
- **Effect**: Turns a punitive levy into a productive seed investment; aligns wealthy interests with local infrastructure success; anchors capital locally.

### 3.5 Phased Opt-In Debt Swap Program (Retail Holders)

Before imposing the haircut, **retail Treasury holders** (individuals, not institutions) may **opt in** to a **Phased Debt Swap**:

- **Eligibility**: Individual holders of marketable Treasuries (direct or via brokerage), up to $500k face value per household.
- **Swap Options** (holder chooses one):
  1. **County LVT Credits**: Face value swapped for pre-paid LVT credits at participating counties (redeemable against future LVT obligations, transferable).
  2. **County TANs**: Face value swapped for County Tax Anticipation Notes (90–180 day, rolling, accepted for local tax payment, circulate as local liquidity).
  3. **Transition Trust Notes**: Face value swapped for 20-year Trust notes (1% coupon) at par, avoiding haircut.
- **Phasing**: Swap window opens Year 0, closes Year 2. After window closes, remaining retail holders subject to standard haircut.
- **Institutional Holders**: Not eligible — subject to standard haircut/Trust note conversion.
- **Effect**: Anchors domestic liquidity locally; creates immediate demand for county revenue instruments (LVT credits, TANs); retail holders avoid haircut; counties gain immediate capital for mutuals/infrastructure.

## 4. Intragovernmental Debt & Unfunded Entitlements — Extinguished

### 4.1 The Accounting Fiction

Social Security and Medicare "trust funds" hold special-issue Treasuries (~$7T) — IOUs from one part of the government to another. They are not assets; they are claims on future tax revenue. **They are extinguished at transition.**

### 4.2 The Real Unfunded Obligation

The ~$7T in trust fund IOUs is an accounting artifact. The **actual 75-year present value shortfall** for the programs those trust funds notionally back is:

| Program                 | 75-Year PV Shortfall | Source               |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Social Security**     | ~$24T                | 2024 Trustees Report |
| **Medicare (HI + SMI)** | ~$50–55T             | 2024 Trustees Report |
| **Total**               | **~$75–80T**         | —                    |

**These are not debts the Confederation inherits.** They are open-ended statutory promises of the old regime. The settlement rule: **accrued benefits for current/near-retirees are honored via the Transition Trust (5-year tail); future accrual stops; workers under 55 transition to county DC plans and health mutuals.** No intergenerational transfer beyond the transition window.

### 4.2 Beneficiary Transition

| Program             | Old Benefit         | New Arrangement                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Social Security** | Monthly check       | **County Pension Trust** (public-finance.md §5): accrued benefit honored at transition, funded by Transition Trust for current retirees; workers <55 transition to county DC plan. |
| **Medicare**        | Fee-for-service     | **County Health Mutual** (healthcare.md): all beneficiaries enrolled automatically. Premiums income-based.                                                                         |
| **Medicaid**        | State-federal match | **County Health Mutual** (same). No means test beyond premium subsidy.                                                                                                             |
| **SSI**             | Means-tested cash   | **County Disability Mutual** (domestic-policy.md §4).                                                                                                                              |
| **CHIP**            | Child coverage      | **County Health Mutual** (children automatic).                                                                                                                                     |

### 4.3 Funding the Transition (Current Beneficiaries)

| Group                     | Treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Funding                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Retirees (65+)**        | Benefit honored at current level, paid by Transition Trust                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Asset sales + levy (5 years)      |
| **Near-retirees (55–64)** | Benefit honored at 80% level, phased to county plan                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Transition Trust + county pension |
| **Workers (<55)**         | Transition to county DC plan; **prior-service credit** (vesting/service recognition only, no dollar value) for documented FICA contributions; accrued benefit preserved as lump-sum or annuity; **Sliding-Scale LVT Transition Voucher** (ages 40–54): 50–54 receive 40% LVT credit voucher; 45–49 receive 25%; 40–44 receive 10%. Vouchers are transferable County LVT tax credits, redeemable against annual LVT obligations over a 10-year window or assignable to county pension trusts as initial capital equity. | County pension trust              |
| **Disabled**              | County disability mutual (no gap)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | County mutual + state reinsurance |

**No "trust fund" survives.** No intergenerational transfer beyond the 5-year transition.

**Age-55 cutoff — deliberate policy boundary with sliding-scale mitigation.** Workers aged 55+ receive Trust-funded benefits for 5 years (transition cohort). Workers under 55 transition to county DC plans with prior-service credit (vesting/service recognition only — no dollar value, no federal backstop). Workers aged 40–54 additionally receive a **Sliding-Scale LVT Transition Voucher**: 50–54 receive a 40% credit voucher; 45–49 receive 25%; 40–44 receive 10%. Vouchers are transferable County LVT tax credits, redeemable against annual LVT obligations over a 10-year window or assignable to county pension trusts as initial capital equity. Vouchers are funded from county land base credit, **not** from Transition Trust cash — net Trust cash outflow remains $0. The hard cutoff at 55 remains a design choice: it bounds Trust liability and forces county-level responsibility for workers under 55. A 54-year-old who paid FICA for 35 years enters the county DC plan fully vested with a 40% LVT credit voucher but receives no federal payment; a 56-year-old receives Trust-funded benefits for 5 years. No phase-in beyond the voucher schedule is specified.

## 6. Veterans' Benefits

### 6.1 Health Care

- **VA hospitals/clinics** → County hospital trusts (asset transfer) or state compacts.
- **Veterans** = county health mutual members (automatic). Service-connected care = mutual covers, county LVT subsidizes.
- **No VA bureaucracy.** No "VA disability rating" for care access.

### 6.2 Disability Compensation (Accrued Liability PV ~$5.3T)

**Tiered by rating severity** — aligns with the occupational injury model for new claims. Only the most severely impaired retain lifetime Trust funding; lower ratings transition to county mutuals or lump sums.

| Rating Tier                                                    | Veterans | Treatment                                                          | Trust PV   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| **100% / IU / SMC** (life-altering, often combat)              | ~500k    | Lifetime Trust annuity, **COLA frozen** at transition CPI          | ~$2.5T     |
| **70–90%** (significant impairment)                            | ~1.2M    | Lifetime Trust annuity, **no COLA** (nominal fixed)                | ~$1.5T     |
| **50–60%** (manageable)                                        | ~1.0M    | **Lump sum** at 80% actuarial value; then county disability mutual | ~$0.8T     |
| **≤40%** (mild)                                                | ~1.5M    | **Lump sum** at 70% actuarial value; then county disability mutual | ~$0.5T     |
| **DIC (survivors)**                                            | ~400k    | County disability mutual (widows/orphans)                          | $0         |
| **Presumptive non-combat** (Agent Orange, Gulf War, burn pits) | ~800k    | County disability mutual (independent medical review)              | $0         |
| **Total Trust PV**                                             | —        | —                                                                  | **~$5.3T** |

| Element                      | Settlement                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **New claims**               | County disability mutual (occupational injury model) + independent medical review                                               |
| **Appeals**                  | County trial court (not VA Board)                                                                                               |
| **Fraud review**             | **Mandatory re-evaluation** of all existing ratings within 24 months; fraudulent/unsupported claims terminated; savings → Trust |
| **Clothing allowance, etc.** | Folded into disability payment                                                                                                  |

**Note**: Baseline PV was ~$7T. Tiered model saves **~$1.7T**. Annual Trust cost drops from ~$150B/yr to **~$75B/yr** (coupon relief ~$17B/yr). Fraud re-evaluation targets the ~45% of budget in DIC/SMC/presumptive categories where exploitation is highest.

### 6.3 Education / Home Loan / Insurance

- **GI Bill** → County education trust (tuition + stipend for veterans, 5 years post-transition).
- **VA home loan** → County mortgage mutual (see §8).
- **SGLI/VGLI** → Private conversion or cash-out.

## 8. Contingent Liabilities (Wound Down)

### 8.1 Deposit Insurance (FDIC/NCUA) — Zero-Gap Bridge

**Core problem**: FDIC ends on Decree Day; new mutuals don't exist yet → guaranteed bank run on the Transition Trust backstop.

**Solution**: Pre-capitalized county deposit mutuals + automatic conversion + TAN-backed narrow banking. No gap, no run.

| Step                                       | Action                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **0. Pre-capitalize (Year 0, pre-Decree)** | Transition Trust allocates **~$500B–1T** from asset sales + seigniorage to charter and capitalize **~3,000 county deposit mutuals** (~$150–300M each). Mutuals hold **100% reserves in County TANs + coin**. |
| **1. Auto-convert (Decree Day)**           | All FDIC/NCUA-insured deposits **automatically convert** to mutual-insured deposits. No depositor action required. Account numbers, routing, access unchanged.                                               |
| **2. Narrow banking (Years 0–2)**          | Mutuals operate as **100% reserve narrow banks**: every insured dollar backed 1:1 by TANs (short-term, LVT-backed) + coin. No lending against insured deposits.                                              |
| **3. Gradual deepening (Years 2–5)**       | Mutuals may shift to fractional reserve with **private reinsurance** (cross-county risk pools). Transition Trust backstop ends at Year 2.                                                                    |
| **4. No federal backstop ever**            | After Year 2, zero taxpayer guarantee. Mutuals are county-chartered, privately reinsured, LVT-backed.                                                                                                        |

**Why this stops the run**:

- **No gap** — mutuals exist and are capitalized _before_ FDIC ends
- **No action needed** — auto-conversion means depositors don't queue to withdraw
- **Full reserve** — 100% TAN/coin backing means zero credit risk on insured deposits
- **Local sovereignty** — mutuals are county-chartered, not federal
- **Competitive currency** — TANs circulate as local liquidity (already in design)

**Funding**: ~$500B–1T from **Quick Wins asset sales (asset-transfer.md §1A.1: SPR crude, gold revaluation, surplus buildings)** + **coin seigniorage (public-finance.md §1)**. This is **<3% of Trust funding** and eliminates the single largest run risk.

**Cross-refs**: `asset-transfer.md §1A.1` (Quick Wins), `public-finance.md §1` (County Deposit Mutuals, TANs), `personnel-transition.md §7.1` (Quick Wins funding).

### 8.2 Mortgage Guarantees (FHA/VA/Ginnie/Fannie/Freddie)

| Step             | Action                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Freeze**    | No new guarantees after Decree Day.                                            |
| **2. Honor**     | Existing guarantees honored (Transition Trust) until loans mature or are sold. |
| **3. Privatize** | County- or state-chartered mortgage mutuals (private insurance) replace.       |
| **4. Wind down** | GSEs (Fannie/Freddie) sold to private investors or liquidated.                 |

### 8.3 Pension Guarantee (PBGC) — Currently ~$60B Surplus

| Step             | Action                                                            |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Honor**     | Existing guaranteed benefits paid by Transition Trust.            |
| **2. Privatize** | County pension trusts (funded, DC) replace. No federal guarantee. |
| **3. Wind down** | PBGC dissolved after existing claims paid.                        |

**Note**: PBGC currently operates at a ~$60B surplus (FY2024). No deficit to fund.

### 8.4 Federal Student Loan Portfolio (~$1.7T Asset)

**This is a federal asset, not a liability.** It is removed from the debt balance sheet.

| Step                  | Action                                                                                                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Freeze**         | No new federal loans after Decree Day.                                                                          |
| **2. Transfer**       | Portfolio sold to county/private lenders at discount (or held by Transition Trust as an asset).                 |
| **3. Discharge**      | Bankruptcy dischargeable (judicial-system.md). No means test.                                                   |
| **4. No forgiveness** | No blanket cancellation (moral hazard). But: income-based repayment capped at 10% for 10 years, then discharge. |

## 9. The Transition Trust (The Vehicle)

### 9.1 Structure

- **Legal form**: Independent trust, chartered by the Decree, governed by 7 sortition-selected trustees + 3 financial experts.
- **Mandate**: Pay legacy claims per this document. No other function.
- **Duration**: 20 years (5-year transition + 15-year tail). **Exempt from the Year 5 sunset** (constitution Art VIII §2; public-finance.md §10); hard end at Year 20.
- **Sunset**: Dissolves automatically at Year 20; residual assets → counties per capita. No perpetual liability.

### 9.2 Funding Sources

| Source                                 | Est.            | Timing     | Notes                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Asset sales**                        | $4–7T           | Years 0–5  | Land, buildings, spectrum, gold, reserves                                                                                                                                   |
| **Seigniorage**                        | $250B           | Years 0–5  | Coin minting profit                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Transition levy**                    | $2.5T           | Years 0–5  | Wealth/income/financial transaction levy (sunsets Year 5)                                                                                                                   |
| **Haircut savings (tiered, on $27T)**  | **$13.6–17.5T** | Year 0     | Fed extinguishment ($5T) + foreign official 60–75% ($4.2–6T) + domestic institutional 40–50% ($4.4–6.5T); retail protected                                                  |
| **Student loan portfolio sale**        | $0.5–1T         | Years 0–3  | Asset sale proceeds (net of discount)                                                                                                                                       |
| **Veterans disability tiered savings** | **~$1.7T**      | Year 0     | 100%/IU/SMC lifetime (COLA frozen), 70-90% lifetime (no COLA), ≤60% lump sum, DIC/presumptive → county mutual                                                               |
| **Deposit mutual pre-capitalization**  | **~$0.5–1T**    | Year 0     | ~3,000 county deposit mutuals @ ~$150–300M each; 100% TAN/coin reserves; auto-converts FDIC deposits on Decree Day                                                          |
| **Trust-note issuance**                | **$15.3–24.3T** | Years 0–20 | **20-year notes**, 1% coupon, funded by county assessment (**0.5% of assessed unimproved land value annually**); coupon **$76–121B/yr** (20-yr amortization)                |
| **Total**                              | **~$32–50T**    |            | Covers: public debt net of tiered haircut + transition cohort benefits (5yr, net of vet savings) + contractor claims + contingent wind-down + deposit mutual capitalization |

**Critical gap**: The ~$75–80T unfunded entitlement PV is **not** funded by the Trust. Only the **transition cohort** (current retirees + near-retirees 55+) receives Trust-funded benefits for 5 years. Workers <55 transition to county DC plans/health mutuals with **no Trust funding**. This is the design's solvency hinge.

### Design Note: Asset Liquidation — Why $4–7T Is the Right Baseline

**The question**: _Are we missing federal assets that could bring more dollars to the Trust?_

**Short answer**: No. The $4–7T baseline (surplus land, buildings, spectrum, gold, reserves) is conservative but correct. Aggressive liquidation would yield +$3–7T _one-time_ but destroys the county asset base that funds the post-Year-5 system.

#### What's Already in the Baseline ($4–7T)

| Asset                            | Proceeds | Method                      |
| -------------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------- |
| Surplus BLM/USFS land (non-park) | $2–5T    | County auctions, Years 1–5  |
| Federal buildings (surplus)      | $500B    | Auction/transfer, Years 1–3 |
| Spectrum (FCC commercial)        | $500B    | Auction, Year 0–1           |

#### Why the Design Chooses Conservative Baseline

1. **County sovereignty needs asset bases** — Counties can't fund pensions/mutuals/infrastructure on LVT alone; they need recurring yield from MBS, loans, spectrum, data.
2. **No federal sales apparatus** — 224-person remnant can't manage $10T+ liquidation.
3. **Vested property rights (Art VI §2.12)** — Patents, copyrights, granted licenses survive transition; can't truncate.
4. **Overlapping claims** — 640M acres have tribal, state, county, conservation claims; litigation ties up value for years.
5. **Political legitimacy** — "Sold the national parks to pay Chinese bondholders" = revolution.
6. **Time horizon** — 5-year Trust window; orderly sales take 3–7 years.

| Base                        | Rate   | Duration |

**Sunset**: Automatic at Year 5. No extension without county referendum.

**Interaction with the state infrastructure surcharge**: The state surcharge (`public-finance.md` §3) is a carved-out share of the county-collected LVT base, not a separate state levy. It may not take effect before Year 2 and is capped at 1% during Years 2–5 while this levy is in force; it rises to 2% only after this levy sunsets at Year 5. The two levies do not share a base, but the sequencing keeps the total transition burden visible and bounded.

### 11.1 No Borrowing Capacity — By Design

The Confederation has **no central bank, no standing army, and no borrowing capacity**. It cannot issue sovereign debt, cannot print money to service external claims, and cannot project force to protect foreign creditors. This is not a gap to be filled; it is the design's core architecture:

- **No lender of last resort.** No Fed, no Treasury, no monetary authority.
- **No military enforcement.** No power to seize foreign assets or retaliate against creditor nations.
- **No successor liability.** The Confederation inherits nothing and assumes nothing.

Consequently, **foreign debt repudiation is the only coherent option.** The 60–75% haircut on foreign official holders is not a negotiating position — it is the inevitable result of institutional reality.

### 11.2 International Trade Settlement — County-to-County, Specie or Barter

With no sovereign debt instruments and no reserve currency, international trade is routed through **direct county-to-county or regional compact trade pacts**, settling balances in **physical gold/silver coin or barter commodities** rather than sovereign debt:

| Element                           | Design                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade pacts**                   | County-to-county or regional compact agreements, voluntary and terminable. No federal trade authority.                      |
| **Settlement medium**             | Physical gold/silver coin, or barter commodities (grain, energy, minerals, manufactured goods).                             |
| **No sovereign debt instruments** | No Treasuries, no bonds, no letters of credit backed by the Confederation.                                                  |
| **No reserve currency role**      | The dollar's reserve status ends by design. No county or compact issues a global reserve asset.                             |
| **Sister-region agreements**      | Counties/states may form direct pacts with foreign regions, bypassing national-level diplomacy (vestigial-functions.md §1). |

This is not autarky. It is **de-dollarized, decentralized trade** — commerce without a central monetary authority, settled in tangible assets rather than promises backed by a vanished state.

### 11.3 Bilateral Commodity-for-Debt Clearing Agreements

Foreign official holders of haircut Treasury debt may elect to settle remaining balances through **commodity-for-debt clearing agreements** rather than Trust notes or coin. These are bilateral agreements between the Transition Trust (or its county/compact designees) and foreign central banks or sovereign wealth funds:

| Element             | Design                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Eligible assets** | Surplus federal land leases (state trust lands), timber harvest rights, mineral extraction licenses, agricultural reserve releases, and strategic commodity stockpiles. |
| **Valuation**       | Asset values assessed at Decree Day market prices; haircut debt balances settled at face value against commodity credits.                                               |
| **Term**            | 10–20 year delivery schedules, renewable by mutual consent.                                                                                                             |
| **Enforcement**     | Administered through county compacts; default results in suspension of delivery, not sovereign litigation.                                                              |
| **No federal lien** | The Confederation holds no title; commodity rights are held by county trusts or state compact entities.                                                                 |

This converts foreign debt claims into productive, long-term commodity relationships without creating new federal debt instruments or assuming new obligations.

### 11.4 Maritime and Air Corridor Reciprocity Treaties

The Diplomatic Receipt Office (vestigial-functions.md §1) may register **bilateral customs and port compacts** with foreign nations:

| Element                 | Design                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Port access**         | Foreign cargo vessels receive tariff-free access to confederal deepwater ports in exchange for recognition of County Travel Documents (CTDs) and protection against foreign asset seizures on confederal territory. |
| **Air corridor**        | Reciprocal overflight rights for foreign and confederal aircraft, subject to county-level airport authority approval.                                                                                               |
| **Customs cooperation** | County-level customs cooperation agreements for container inspection, contraband interdiction, and shared intelligence.                                                                                             |
| **No federal treaty**   | These are county/compact-level compacts, not Confederation treaties; they bind only the participating counties and foreign ports.                                                                                   |

These compacts preserve trade corridors without creating a federal foreign policy apparatus.

### 11.5 Sovereign Reset Declaration

The Confederation **does not inherit, assume, or guarantee** any foreign debt obligation of the prior regime. Foreign dollar reserves held as Treasury securities are **legally extinguished** as unbacked artifacts of the prior regime's monetary architecture. The Confederation:

- Has **no central treasury** that foreign creditors can attach in foreign courts.
- Has **no sovereign immunity** to waive, as it is not a successor state.
- Has **no borrowing capacity** and cannot issue new debt instruments.
- Has **no standing army** to enforce or defend foreign claims.

Foreign holders of haircut Treasury debt receive Trust notes (1% coupon, 20-year maturity) or commodity-for-debt clearing agreements (see §11.3) in exchange for their claims. Beyond that, no further obligation exists. The dollar reserve system ends by design; its remnants are settled once and for all at transition.

## 15. The Honest Bottom Line

**The old regime is insolvent.** Its promises (~$60T+) exceed its assets (~$5T). No settlement can pay everyone in full without a successor state that taxes forever.

**This settlement:**

- Pays private creditors (mostly) to preserve the financial system.
- Extinguishes intragovernmental fiction (trust funds).
- Honors accrued pensions (transition cohort) but stops future accrual.
- Wounds down contingent guarantees (no perpetual backstop).
- Funds the gap with asset sales + a 5-year levy + 20-year Trust notes.
- **Leaves no successor liable.**

**The cost is borne by:** wealthy holders (levy), bondholders (haircut), future retirees (transition to DC), and the general public (5 years of reduced services + asset sales). **No one is made whole. Everyone knows the rules in advance.**

---

**The system is done when:** Every legacy claim is either paid, haircut, extinguished, or privatized — by Year 20 — and the Confederation's balance sheet is empty. No debt. No trust funds. No guarantees. No unfunded liabilities. **A clean slate, funded by the old regime's own assets, with the losses allocated by explicit rule rather than inflation or default-by-stealth.**
